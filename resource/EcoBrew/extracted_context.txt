ANNUAL SUSTAINABILITY PERFORMANCE & 2025 GOAL SCORECARD
EcoBrew Annual Sustainability Report (Fiscal Year
2025)
Comprehensive ESG Auditing, Operational KPIs, and Scorecard Reconciliation
I. THE 2025 SUSTAINABILITY TARGETS (THE "FOUR PILLARS")
In 2020, EcoBrew established four core sustainability pillars with target deadlines set for year-end 2025:
 Water Stewardship: Target 100% of facilities in high-stress basins operating with watershed action plans,
achieving global average water usage below 2.50 hl/hl (and high-stress sites below 2.00 hl/hl).
 Smart Agriculture: Target 100% of direct contracted farmers skilled, connected, and empowered with
regenerative practices and financial tools.
 Circular Packaging: Target 100% of product packaging returnable or made from majority-recycled content.

 Climate Action: Target 100% purchased electricity from renewable sources, and cut operational GHG emissions
intensity across Scopes 1 and 2 by 30%.
II. 2025 SCORECARD EVALUATION: ACHIEVED VERSUS MISSED TARGETS
1. Targets Successfully Achieved:
 Water Stewardship (Achieved): Global average water usage ratio fell to 2.32 hl/hl (surpassing 2.50 hl/hl goal). In
high-stress watershed breweries (Cascade Brewery in Mexico and Central Valley Brewery in California), the ratio
reached 1.94 hl/hl (surpassing 2.00 hl/hl threshold).
 Smart Agriculture (Achieved): 100% of direct contracted farmers (over 4,200 agricultural producers across 11
nations) actively onboarded into the regenerative agriculture platform.
 Climate Action Intensity (Achieved): Reduced operational Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions intensity by
32.4% against baseline (exceeding 30% reduction goal).
 Contracted Renewable Electricity (Achieved): 100% of global purchased electricity matched with long-term
renewable contracts.
2. Targets Missed:
 Circular Packaging (MISSED): EcoBrew achieved 89.7% circular packaging against its 100% goal. While
returnable glass bottle systems achieved 96.9% returnable volume in prime markets (Germany, Austria, Mexico),
the overall metric was pulled down by severe global market shortages of food-grade recycled polyethylene
terephthalate (rPET) resin and municipal sorting bottlenecks.
 Operational Renewable Electricity (MISSED): Physical operational renewable electricity utilization reached
84.5% against 100% operational target due to delayed electrical grid interconnection queues and transformer
hardware delivery lead times.

III. CORPORATE VENTURING AND THE CIRCULAR ECONOMY:
CIRCULARGRAIN FOODS
In traditional brewing, spent barley grain represents the brewing process's largest solid waste byproduct. Globally,
EcoBrew generates over 1.4 million metric tons of wet spent grain annually.
In 2021, EcoBrew's corporate venturing arm, EcoVentures, seeded an internal startup named CircularGrain Foods
with an initial investment of $4.5 million, followed by an additional $110 million in growth capital to construct
specialized protein-refining processing plants adjacent to major breweries in Portland, Oregon and Ghent, Belgium.
CircularGrain Foods developed a proprietary mechanical wet-milling and enzymatic separation technology that
transforms wet spent barley into two high-value commercial ingredients:
 EcoVita: An insoluble, prebiotic dietary fiber tailored for commercial baking, snacks, and digestive nutrition
products.
 EcoPro: A highly soluble, neutral-tasting plant protein isolate containing all nine essential amino acids, formulated
for dairy-alternative beverages, sports nutrition shakes, and plant-based foods.
By 2024, CircularGrain Foods upcycled 180,000 metric tons of spent grain, averted 64,000 metric tons of landfill
methane emissions, and generated $35 million in high-margin B2B commercial revenue, validating Streamline Two
as a self-sustaining commercial model.
IV. REGENERATIVE AGRICULTURE & THE SOIL HEALTH PROTOCOL
To address the 24.1% of emissions originating in agricultural supply chains, EcoBrew partnered with the Global Soil
Alliance to establish the Five Pillars of Soil Health:
1. Integrated Soil Fertility Management: Balancing organic compost with precision inputs to eliminate nitrogen
runoff.
2. Minimum Conservation Tillage: Preserving mycelial soil networks, preventing wind erosion, and retaining
organic soil carbon.
3. Multispecies Cover Cropping: Planting non-commercial cover crops between barley cycles to fix nitrogen
naturally.
4. Diverse Crop Rotation Systems: Mandating 3-to-4 crop rotation cycles alternating barley with legumes,
brassicas, and oilseeds.
5. Watershed-Level Biodiversity Buffers: Dedicated native wildflower and riparian tree buffer zones along
waterways.
V. CORPORATE GOVERNANCE AND INCENTIVE STRUCTURES
EcoBrew's ESG strategy is governed by the Sustainability Executive Council, established in 2018. Chaired by Chief
Sustainability & Supply Officer Marcus Thorne, the council includes the CFO, CMO, Chief Legal Officer, and Zone
EVPs. The Council convenes bi-monthly and reports directly to the Board of Directors biannually. Over 450 global
executive and senior operational leaders have up to 25% of their annual variable performance compensation tied
directly to audited ESG milestones.

CASE CODE: HBS-EB-2024-08
EcoBrew Artisan Beverages: Scaling Sustainable
Brewing in an Age of Disruption
Prepared by Professor Marcus Vance and Research Fellow Sarah Jenkins (Harvard SEAS)
I. EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW & CORPORATE PROFILE
EcoBrew Artisan Beverages ("EcoBrew") is one of the world's leading beverage corporations, operating a portfolio of
over 240 artisan, premium, and craft beverage brands across North America, Europe, Latin America, and the
Asia-Pacific region. Founded in 1998 as a cooperative craft brewery in Portland, Oregon, EcoBrew underwent twenty
years of disciplined domestic expansion and strategic international acquisitions.
By 2024, the enterprise employed approximately 128,000 people across 42 countries, operating 86 industrial
breweries, 14 craft lighthouse innovation breweries, and 18 regional distribution hubs.
In 2024, EcoBrew reported annual consolidated revenue of $54.6 billion with net income of $5.4 billion, solidifying its
ranking among the top consumer packaged goods (CPG) companies globally. Despite its rapid commercial ascent,
EcoBrew faces intense operational exposure to climate disruption, raw agricultural commodity volatility (particularly
barley and hops), municipal water basin scarcity, and tightening international greenhouse gas (GHG) reporting
mandates.
Under Chief Executive Officer Elena Vance and Chief Sustainability & Supply Officer Marcus Thorne, the company
established an overarching corporate purpose: "Brewing for Tomorrow: Purity, Craft, and Planetary Resilience." To
translate this purpose into measurable operating results, executive leadership introduced an operational flywheel
based on the concept that operational lean discipline and continuous waste elimination generate the working capital
required to self-fund breakthrough environmental transformation.
II. THE DUAL SUPPLY CHAIN STRATEGY: STREAMLINE ONE AND
STREAMLINE TWO
A cornerstone of EcoBrew's operational philosophy is its ambidextrous supply chain architecture, formally divided into
two interconnected engines: "Streamline One" and "Streamline Two."
1. Streamline One (The Efficiency Engine):
Streamline One represents EcoBrew's core linear manufacturing, procurement, and physical distribution network. It
focuses rigorously on short-term operational excellence, lean shop-floor management, predictive maintenance,
automated high-speed canning and bottling lines, and unit cost minimization. Through standard operating procedures
(SOPs) and computerized line analytics, Streamline One delivers steady gross margin improvements, eliminating
$680 million in cumulative operational waste between 2021 and 2024.
Confidential — For Business School Discussion & Case Evaluation Only

2. Streamline Two (The Sustainability and Circularity Engine):
Streamline Two is the digital, interconnected, and circular supply chain of the future. Rather than seeking separate
external venture debt or issuing dilutive equity to fund decarbonization, EcoBrew routes direct cost savings extracted
by Streamline One into Streamline Two's sustainability projects. Streamline Two manages closed-loop circular
packaging, renewable energy deployments, regenerative agricultural partnerships, watershed protection, and the
corporate venturing incubator (EcoVentures).
"Streamline One and Streamline Two are not competing priorities. If Streamline One fails to optimize
shop-floor yields, Streamline Two loses its capital pipeline. Conversely, if Streamline Two fails to protect our
water basins and barley supply, Streamline One will run out of clean raw materials to brew beer." — Marcus
Thorne, Chief Sustainability & Supply Officer
III. MANUFACTURING INFRASTRUCTURE & LIGHTHOUSE BREWERIES
EcoBrew's production network balances mega-scale industrial output with agile experimental facilities:
 Cascade Brewery (Guadalajara, Mexico): EcoBrew's largest single manufacturing hub, employing over 2,800
workers with an annual nameplate capacity of 18 million hectoliters. The facility bottles 16 million glass bottles and
5 million aluminum cans per day. Situated in a semi-arid plateau experiencing severe multi-year drought, Cascade
serves as the primary testbed for advanced water recycling and solar thermal brewing.
 Bend Lighthouse Brewery (Bend, Oregon): A state-of-the-art facility producing 450,000 hectoliters annually.
Bend operates as EcoBrew's primary agile research laboratory, where master brewers, machine-learning
engineers, and material scientists test prototype packaging, novel yeast strains, and low-thermal-energy brewing
vessels before broad factory rollout.

OFFICIAL SBTi-ALIGNED 2040 DECARBONIZATION ROADMAP
EcoBrew Net-Zero Transition Plan (2024–2040)
Validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi) under the 1.5°C Corporate Net-Zero
Standard
I. GREENHOUSE GAS INVENTORY AND BASELINE BOUNDARIES
In accordance with the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard, EcoBrew conducted
comprehensive greenhouse gas emissions accounting across its global operating footprint.
1. Overall Emissions Distribution:
 Scope 1 (Direct Operations): 6.8% of total footprint (combustion of natural gas in boilers, on-site fleet fuels,
refrigerant leakage).
 Scope 2 (Purchased Electricity & Steam): 8.0% of total footprint (market-based emissions from municipal
electric utilities and district heating).
 Scope 3 (Value Chain Upstream & Downstream): 85.2% of total corporate carbon footprint.

2. Scope 3 Value Chain Granular Breakdown:
Because Scope 3 accounts for 85.2% of total emissions, EcoBrew identified four primary carbon-intensive asset
classes across its upstream suppliers and downstream customer touchpoints:
 Packaging Materials & Manufacturing (38.4% of total): The single largest emissions driver, driven by bauxite
smelting into aluminum cans, high-temperature glass melting furnaces, and virgin PET plastic resin synthesis.
 Agricultural Raw Materials (24.1% of total): Synthetic nitrogen fertilizer application, soil nitrous oxide emissions,
tractor diesel, and contracted barley/hop malting processes.
 Downstream Point-of-Sale Product Cooling & Dispense (17.5% of total): Commercial beverage display
chillers, bar draught dispense refrigeration, and retail cold-vault units operated by independent retailers.
 Logistics, Freight, and Warehousing (5.2% of total): Ocean freight, third-party diesel trucking, and contracted
intermodal transport networks.
II. CORE DECARBONIZATION LEVERS
1. Breakthrough Thermal Brewing Innovation ("HydroVapor Recuperation"):
Traditional brewing wort boiling requires immense thermal steam, typically generated by fossil natural gas boilers.
EcoBrew engineering teams invented and patented HydroVapor Recuperation (HVR). HVR bubbles micro-jets of
inert ambient nitrogen gas through the wort boiling stage, lowering boiling thermal heat requirements while preserving
volatile hop aromas and stripping unwanted dimethyl sulfide (DMS).
 Energy Reduction: HVR reduces kettle thermal heat requirements by 65% per hectoliter brewed.

 Open Intellectual Property Policy: EcoBrew chose not to restrict HVR behind proprietary licensing fees. Instead,
EcoBrew published open blueprints and licensed the patented HVR system free of charge to any craft or
independent brewery worldwide, recognizing that sector-wide decarbonization creates far greater ESG value
while EcoBrew's true moat resides in brand affinity and distribution scale.
Confidential — For Business School Discussion & Case Evaluation Only

2. Biomass Gasification and Renewable Thermal Energy:
At the Cascade Valley Malthouse in Oregon, EcoBrew converted 100% of fossil natural gas kilns to closed-loop
biomass gasifiers powered by agricultural waste byproducts (spent barley chaff, oat hulls, fruit pomace). The
installation cut facility thermal emissions by 92%, making Cascade Valley the enterprise's first certified carbon-neutral
malthouse.
3. Sourcing 100% Renewable Electricity:
EcoBrew set a target to achieve 100% renewable electricity across all global manufacturing facilities by 2025. By
year-end 2025, EcoBrew achieved 100% contracted renewable electricity through off-site Power Purchase
Agreements (PPAs). Actual physical operational renewable power utilization reached 84.5% (missed the 100%
operational target due to delayed electrical grid interconnection queues and transformer supply bottlenecks in
developing markets).
4. Downstream Retail Point-of-Sale Cooling Decarbonization:
Because commercial beverage refrigeration contributes 17.5% of total carbon footprint, EcoBrew launched two
structured interventions for third-party storefronts:
 The SolBar Initiative (Mexico): Financed and installed turnkey rooftop solar PV arrays across 18,500 partner
corner storefronts and taprooms with zero upfront capital cost to shopkeepers.
 VerdeGrid Energy (Europe & South America): Bundled clean electricity procurement for 65,000 small
independent taverns, abating over 190,000 metric tons of CO2e annually while cutting retailers' monthly power
bills by 14%.
 CryoChill Acoustic Chillers: Deployed vacuum acoustic-wave chilling cycles that chill bottled beverages eight
times faster than standard commercial compressor refrigeration while consuming 18% less electrical power.
5. Sustainable Green Logistics:
EcoBrew announced binding purchase agreements for 850 heavy-duty battery-electric Class 8 distribution trucks
from CleanFleet with depot charging at 12 primary distribution hubs, alongside a regional pilot of 40 hydrogen fuel-cell
tractors in northern Europe.
6. Strict Carbon Offset Boundary Policy:
EcoBrew's SBTi-validated Net Zero 2040 commitment strictly prohibits using voluntary carbon offsets or carbon
credits to claim operational carbon neutrality or net-zero progress. Under EcoBrew policy, carbon emissions must be
eliminated through direct operational abatement, equipment electrification, energy efficiency, and value-chain
supplier decarbonization. Carbon offsets are authorized solely as a temporary compensation mechanism for residual,
technically unabated emissions starting no earlier than 2038.
